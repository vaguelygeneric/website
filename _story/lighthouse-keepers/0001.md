The supply boat left before I could ask the name of the island, and by the time I thought to shout after it, the fog had already taken the shape of an answer I didn't like.

There is a lighthouse here. That much the boat captain confirmed, tapping the chart twice as though the paper needed convincing. What he did not confirm — what he seemed, in fact, actively unwilling to discuss — is why the light does not appear on any chart from the mainland side, despite being visible, by my own count, on nine of the last ten nights.

The tenth night, naturally, was the night I arrived.

I've spent the morning doing what any reasonable keeper would do: counting the stairs. There are one hundred and forty-two, which is one more than the tower's height would seem to allow, and one fewer than a groove worn into the fourth-floor landing suggests someone once believed. I have not yet decided whose count to trust — mine, or the stairs'.

The lamp room is in working order, if "order" can describe a mechanism that hums a half-tone flat and casts a light I have twice now caught, from the outside, appearing to originate about eleven feet to the left of the actual lighthouse.

I will write again once I understand the eleven feet.

— K.
