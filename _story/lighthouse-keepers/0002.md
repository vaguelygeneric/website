I measured it properly this time, with a line and a stake, in daylight, while the light was off and had no opinion on the matter.

Eleven feet, four inches, due west of the tower's actual base — that is where the beam originates, according to three separate nights of triangulation from the rocks below. I have ruled out fog, refraction, a second and unlisted lighthouse, and my own eyesight, in that order, and in that order because each was easier to disprove than the last.

What remains is this: either the lighthouse is not where the lighthouse is, or something in the water has learned to imitate it very precisely, eleven feet to the left, for reasons of its own.

I raised the matter with the gull colony on the north rocks, who did not answer, which I am choosing to interpret as professional discretion rather than ignorance. They have, after all, been here considerably longer than the stairs have.

Tomorrow I mean to walk out to the eleven feet at low tide and see what, if anything, is standing there when the light is lit and I am not looking at the tower to compare.

I recognize this is not what the almanac is for. I am keeping it anyway. Someone should.

— K.
